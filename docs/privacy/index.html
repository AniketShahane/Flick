Flick — Privacy Policy

Covers Flick (com.flick.sender, phone) and Flick TV (com.flick.receiver, Android TV).

Effective 7 August 2026

Flick has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no crash reporting. Your videos are sent from your phone to your TV over your own Wi-Fi and never travel through any server we run — because we do not run one.

What Flick does with your videos

Flick plays the video files already on your phone on a TV on the same Wi-Fi network. The phone runs a small web server that the TV reads the file from directly. The original file is sent, byte for byte, from your phone to your TV.

That transfer stays on your local network. Nothing about it is uploaded, copied, relayed, or stored anywhere else. Neither app sends video, audio, thumbnails, or file names to us or to any third party.

Access to your videos on the phone

Flick asks for permission to read the videos on your device so it can show you a browsable library — folders, thumbnails, durations, search, and where you left off in each film. It reads that library only to display it and to serve a file you choose to your TV.

Flick also reads subtitle files sitting next to a video, so a film you already have subtitles for plays with them. It reads no other files, and no photos.

Camera

The phone camera is used for one thing: reading the pairing code shown on the TV. Frames are analysed on the device by a barcode reader bundled inside the app, are never written to storage, and never leave the phone. The camera is only active while the scanner is open, and pairing codes can always be typed by hand instead — Flick installs and works on a phone with no camera.

Subtitle search (optional)

If you search for subtitles online, Flick sends a request to OpenSubtitles (api.opensubtitles.com). That request contains only what is needed to find a match:

  • a search term — the film title, taken from the file name or typed by you;
  • the year, season, and episode number when you supply them;
  • a short numeric fingerprint computed from the video file, which identifies a specific release. It is not the file, and the file cannot be reconstructed from it.

You may optionally sign in to your own OpenSubtitles account to raise your download quota. If you do, the username and password you type are sent to OpenSubtitles to obtain a session token. The password is not stored; the session token and username are kept on your device and are removed when you sign out. Flick never sees or stores your OpenSubtitles password anywhere else.

OpenSubtitles is an independent service with its own privacy policy, which governs what it does with a request it receives. This step is entirely optional — Flick plays your videos, with subtitle files you already have, without it.

Supporting the developer (optional)

Flick is free and unlocks nothing for payment. If you choose to leave a tip, Flick opens a Stripe-hosted checkout page in your browser. Any name, email, or card details you enter there are given to Stripe, not to Flick — the app never sees them and receives nothing back but the fact that you closed the page.

What is stored on your devices

On the phone:

  • your settings;
  • which TV you have paired with, and the key that pairing is built on;
  • where you left off in videos you have played;
  • your OpenSubtitles session token and username, only if you signed in.

On the TV:

  • which phones are paired with it, and their pairing keys;
  • the TV's display name and its playback settings.

All of this stays on the device that holds it. Uninstalling the app deletes it. Pairings can be removed at any time from the TV's Settings screen without uninstalling anything.

What leaves your devices

Flick TV makes no connection to the internet at all. It talks only to a paired phone on your local network.

Flick on the phone connects to exactly one internet host, and only when you ask it to search for subtitles: api.opensubtitles.com. Tapping a tip link hands you off to Stripe in your browser. There is no other outbound connection, no telemetry, and no third-party analytics, advertising, or crash-reporting library in either app.

Children

Flick is not directed at children and collects nothing that would identify anyone, of any age.
